# Accuracy exposes what test-set selection cannot

None of our measurements, and none of the careful published results, close the remaining gap of 0.036 QWK and 13 accuracy points to Chilukoti et al. 2024. Choosing cut-offs, members and subsets on the test set lifts our 0.918 blend to only 0.928. A plausible lucky split adds about another 0.01–0.02. None of these selection effects raises accuracy above 0.83, against the paper's 0.942. Two explanations remain that could produce both gaps at once. The first is a reporting or metric error, and the paper's own EyePACS tables demonstrably contain them. The second is an evaluation set that overlaps training at the level of near-identical copies, the classic result of splitting after offline augmentation. The second can be tested in our pipeline and the first cannot. Our models are also far stronger than the paper's stated recipe. Run under our strict protocol, that recipe scored 0.827 QWK and 0.771 accuracy (`dr-repro/results/leakage_s2_faithful.json`). So the gap that actually needs explaining is closer to 0.13 QWK and 17 accuracy points. Spend the remaining ~9 Kaggle T4 GPU-hours on discrimination, not on another thousandth of QWK. First, about 2.5 h on a paired experiment that splits before versus after augmentation using the paper's own recipe, plus free CPU-only simulations of the lucky-split effect. Second, about 2 h on a corrected low-learning-rate fine-tune from the saved EyePACS weights. Third, if budget remains, about 3 h on fold-safe pseudo-labelling. All of this is post-test work and must be reported in a separate section labelled as such.

## Every lever we can measure stops at 0.928

The modelling side is exhausted. Over Stages 4b and 4c, the levers that worked on validation lifted the strict test QWK from 0.898 to 0.918 (95% CI 0.900–0.935), with OOF 0.928. Those levers were learning rate, strong augmentation, label cleaning, Ben Graham pre-processing, Noisy Student B4, 512 px and 5-fold ensembles. ConvNeXt-T and SE-ResNeXt50 failed their predictions, and the EyePACS arm came out worse than ImageNet. The paired bootstrap of Stage 4c over Stage 4b is +0.003 [−0.003, +0.009], so the last stage was already inside the noise (`dr-repro/stage4c_results.md`).

The literature puts the ceiling in the same place. Fang, Kornblith and Schmidt fine-tuned 19 ImageNet models on a local 80/20 APTOS split, and the best (ConvNeXt-Tiny) reached **0.9297**. That figure is already the maximum over a hyperparameter grid scored on the reporting split ([Fang et al., arXiv 2301.04644](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.04644)). SSiT uses the protocol closest to ours: a random 70/15/15 split, about 550 test images and 5 repeats. Even with self-supervised pre-training on all of EyePACS, it reaches **0.9297 ± 0.0029** ([Huang et al., arXiv 2210.10969](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.10969v5)). The only APTOS-train QWKs above 0.95 with a known protocol come from the 54th-place Kaggle team: **0.969–0.971** on a single 736-image holdout, using EyePACS pre-training plus IDRiD and Messidor. The same system scored only **0.925** on Kaggle's hidden private test ([Tymchenko et al., arXiv 2003.02261](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2003.02261)). Even the competition winner, with about 88,700 extra EyePACS images and an 8-model ensemble, scored **0.936** on that hidden test ([Fang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.04644)).

The paper's stated method contains nothing that should beat us. Its 0.954 row is a single EfficientNet-B3 at 300×300 with CLAHE and Gaussian blur, a softmax head decoded by argmax, Adam at 1e-3 with weight decay 1e-4 and gradient clipping 0.1. It mentions no geometric augmentation, no TTA and no cut-off tuning ([Chilukoti et al. 2024, PMC11323616](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11323616/)). Our Stage 2 run of that recipe under a strict split gave 0.827 QWK and 0.771 accuracy. That was one run, with unstated details filled in by us.

Selection on the test set accounts for only a small part of the gap. The table collects what we have measured on our own models and what can be estimated.

| Candidate source of the gap | Effect on reported QWK | Effect on accuracy | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cut-offs tuned on test (4c blend) | +0.006 | +0.020 (0.807 → 0.827) | measured, `stage4c_results.md` |
| Best of 63 member subsets + cut-offs, both chosen on test | +0.010 (to 0.928) | −0.016 (0.791) | measured |
| Cut-offs tuned on test (weaker Stage 4 models) | +0.008 to +0.024 | not recorded | measured, `protocol_gap.csv` |

Stacking the measured selection ceiling (0.928) with a favourable draw of split gives roughly **0.938–0.945**. These effects overlap, so even that range is generous. A strict-protocol model at our level would still be about 0.01–0.02 short, and its accuracy would not move. Reaching 0.954 from a true value near 0.918 by sampling luck alone needs a draw about four standard deviations above the mean, which is implausible without many tries. One caveat: the paper's test size is unknown. If it held 10% of the data (about 366 images), the sampling SD would be closer to 0.011, and the luck term grows a little.

## Thirteen accuracy points is a signature that selection cannot forge

Accuracy is the sharper diagnostic. QWK forgives off-by-one errors on an imbalanced 5-grade scale, and accuracy does not. Every selection effect we measured moves QWK by a few thousandths and leaves accuracy between **0.79 and 0.83**. Our best-QWK optimistic row (0.928) has the *lowest* accuracy in the table, 0.791. Published models with a stated protocol cluster in the same range. At QWK near 0.93, MIL-VT reports 85.5% 5-class accuracy, RadFuse 87.07% and nnMobileNet 89.1% ([nnMobileNet, arXiv 2306.01289](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.01289); [RadFuse, arXiv 2504.15883](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.15883)). A RETFound-DINOv2 ViT-L fine-tuned on a 733-image APTOS test split reports **0.910 QWK at 79.54% accuracy**, almost exactly our operating point ([arXiv 2605.14108](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.14108v1)). Chilukoti's 94.2% sits 5 points above the best of these and 11 points above any model we have trained.

Label noise makes 94% accuracy suspicious, not just unusual. A Google study measured agreement between DR graders at kappa of only about 0.40–0.65 ([arXiv 1710.01711](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1710.01711)). Inside APTOS itself, a top-3% Kaggle team found that overwriting predictions on leaked duplicate test images with their training labels made the score "drop significantly". They concluded the annotations "were pretty bad" ([MamatShamshiev](https://github.com/MamatShamshiev/Kaggle-APTOS-2019-Blindness-Detection)). Our own duplicate audit found 30 of 123 identical groups carrying conflicting grades. In our Stage 2 run, accuracy on the leaked twins was only 65.5% (`dr-repro/results/leakage_s2_faithful.json`). For a model to agree with 94% of APTOS test labels, it would have to reproduce much of this grader noise. The most direct way to do that is to have seen near-copies of the test images, with their labels, during training. This is why exact-duplicate leakage, which we measured and which does nothing, is the wrong test of the leakage hypothesis. Twins with conflicting grades cannot be memorised consistently. Augmented copies of a single image, all carrying the same label, can.

The steelman deserves a hearing. The authors might have used a clean split and simply had a better model on an easier draw. Two facts weigh against this. The stated recipe scored 0.827 under our strict protocol, so the paper would need about +0.13 QWK from unstated choices. And no carefully evaluated ImageNet-only result in the literature exceeds about 0.934. The remaining candidates are therefore a reporting error or an evaluation set that overlaps training. Our pipeline can distinguish between these only through experiment.

## The paper's tables fail their own arithmetic

For APTOS, the paper states no split, no split ratio, no test-set size, no stratification, no checkpoint rule, no epoch count and no duplicate handling. The words "split", "test set", "duplicate", "threshold" and "seed" never appear in it. The only three-way split it describes, 24,590/5,268/5,268, belongs to EyePACS ([PMC11323616](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11323616/)). There is no code, no supplement and no code-availability statement. The first author's 16 public GitHub repositories contain no DR code ([GitHub, Saivenkatesh951](https://github.com/Saivenkatesh951?tab=repositories)). Because the paper used 3,662 images, its APTOS "test" set must have been carved out of the labelled Kaggle training set by an unstated procedure.

Where the paper does print raw predictions, the numbers do not follow from them. Recomputing standard QWK from the EyePACS confusion matrices in Tables 4–5 gives the following ([PMC11323616](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11323616/)):

- The ensemble's matrix gives **0.919**, not the 0.867 reported in Table 3.
- EfficientNet-B5's matrix gives **0.488**, not 0.
- ResNet-101 and ResNet-152 predict only class 0, so their QWK must be exactly 0, yet the table reports 0.009 and 0.067.

The test set is not fixed either. Each model's matrix holds a different number of images, from **5,008 to 5,339**, with class-0 counts from 3,638 to 3,913, although the stated test size is 5,268. That pattern fits a fresh random split per run, evaluation on different subsets, or matrices assembled from different runs. In none of these cases was there a single fixed hold-out. The text gives an AUC of 0.2 for constant predictors, which is impossible because such a predictor scores 0.5. A global substitution also turned the paper's Kaggle URLs into "https://www.Eyepacs.com/aroraaman/…", which shows how little editorial care the manuscript received ([PMC11323616](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11323616/)).

Table 6, which holds the 0.954, has the same problem, though less clearly. For any per-class or support-weighted averaging, F1 cannot exceed the mean of precision and recall. Under weighted averaging, recall must also equal accuracy. The APTOS/EyePACS/No row reports F1 0.954 against (0.952 + 0.942)/2 = 0.947, and recall 0.942 against accuracy 0.954. Both are impossible under standard averaging. The headline APTOS/None/No row (accuracy 0.942, P 0.943, R 0.943, F1 0.944) also breaks both rules, but only at the third decimal. On its own that could be a rounding or averaging quirk. The 0.954 has no confusion matrix, so it cannot be recomputed. The fair conclusion is narrower than "the number is wrong". The reporting process that produced it gets other numbers wrong in the same paper, and nothing guarantees this one.

The number is nonetheless spreading. The paper has about 43 citations, and none we found re-runs it ([Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1186/s12911-024-02446-x)). One 2026 IEEE paper calls its 0.967 "the upper bound for this architecture class". In the same passage, that paper remarks that high-QWK APTOS methods "do not verify whether their threshold selection was performed on held-out data" ([DOI 10.1109/ICATI69399.2026.11604250](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICATI69399.2026.11604250)). Only the authors can supply the APTOS split, test size, checkpoint rule and confusion matrix. Writing to the corresponding author listed in the paper, Xiali Hei, costs no GPU time. It is the one step that could settle the question outright.

## Splitting after augmentation is the one mechanism big enough to test

Leakage from the same source image appearing on both sides of a split is large wherever it has been measured. In brain MRI, splitting by 2D slice rather than by subject raised test accuracy by **29–55 points**. On randomly labelled OASIS data it still produced about 96% accuracy, against 50% for a subject-level split. The same paper names the "late split", augmenting before separating the test set, as a mechanism for this inflation ([Yagis et al., Sci Rep 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8604922/)). In ophthalmic OCT, improper per-image splits inflated accuracy by **5–30 points** (MCC by 0.07–0.43) ([Tampu et al., Sci Data 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9500039/)). A 2026 migraine-MRI preprint found that SMOTE applied before partitioning "resulted in artificially inflated accuracy values" ([arXiv 2605.23453](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.23453)). Leakage of this kind appears in hundreds of papers across 17 fields ([Kapoor & Narayanan, Patterns 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10499856/)). No one has measured it for fundus grading.

The paper's wording leaves room for it. It describes CLAHE and Gaussian blur as "data augmentation techniques … used to address data limitations" ([PMC11323616](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11323616/)). Elsewhere it calls them pre-processing applied to every image, so this remains a hypothesis. The arithmetic shows why the mechanism matches the signature.

Suppose APTOS were balanced to about 1,805 images per class by offline copies. That means about ×4.9 for grade 1, ×9.4 for grade 3 and ×6.1 for grade 4. After an 80/20 random split, a test copy with k siblings has at least one sibling in training with probability 1 − 0.2^(k−1). That is about **0.998 for k = 5** ([inflation analysis, after Yagis et al.](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8604922/)).

Nearly every test image from the minority grades would then be "seen", and grades 1, 3 and 4 are exactly where strict models make most of their errors. Fixing those errors raises accuracy far more than QWK, which is already near saturation. A leaky split is the only mechanism found that predicts the paper's unusual combination of accuracy just below QWK, both above 0.94. It is a sufficiency test, not proof. If the leaky split reproduces 0.95/0.94 with the paper's recipe, that shows the paper's numbers *can* arise this way, not that they did.

## Spend the nine hours on discrimination, not on another thousandth

The test set has been opened once. Any result from here on is post-test, cannot be confirmed on those 550 images, and needs its own clearly labelled section. The paired-bootstrap width on 550 images is about ±0.006–0.009. That is larger than the expected gain of every remaining modelling lever: B5-NS 456 (+0.001 to +0.004, about 3 h), FixRes fine-tuning, extra seeds, and RETFound or DINOv2 partial fine-tunes. A RETFound-DINOv2 model scored 0.910 QWK on APTOS ([arXiv 2605.14108](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.14108v1)). FLAIR must be excluded outright, because its pre-training corpus includes APTOS ([FLAIR repo](https://github.com/jusiro/FLAIR)). The budget should go to experiments whose outcome changes the explanation in the write-up.

| Priority | Experiment (all post-test) | T4 time | Pre-registered expectation and decision rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | CPU-only protocol simulations on saved predictions: QWK and accuracy over 1,000 random stratified 550-image draws from the 3,112 honest OOF predictions; expected maximum over N = 5, 10, 20 draws; validation-as-test scores | 0 | Measures the "lucky split" term on our own model instead of estimating it; expect +0.010 to +0.017 on QWK and under +0.02 on accuracy |
| 1 | Split before versus after augmentation, using the paper's own recipe | ~2.5 h | Leaky arms reach QWK ≥ 0.95 and accuracy ≥ 0.92 while the clean arm stays near 0.83 / 0.77 → mechanism sufficient. Leaky accuracy < 0.90 → mechanism insufficient, and reporting error becomes the leading account |
| 2 | Corrected EyePACS transfer: low-lr fine-tune from the saved 5-epoch weights | ~2 h | OOF ≥ 0.9195 (ImageNet B4-NS 380) with no dip in epochs 5–7 → forgetting confirmed. Expected +0.000 to +0.008. External-data row only |
| 3 | Fold-safe soft pseudo-labelling of the 1,928 Kaggle test images, warm-started B3 384 and B4-NS 380 students | ~3 h | +0.002 to +0.008 OOF, centred near +0.004; row labelled "strict labels + unlabelled competition images" |
| — | Reserve for overruns or a second seed of the leaky arm | ~1.5 h | |

**Experiment 1 design.** Reuse the Stage 2 faithful recipe exactly: B3 at 300 px, CLAHE + Gaussian blur, a softmax head with argmax, Adam at 1e-3, weight decay 1e-4 and gradient clipping 0.1. Then only the order of splitting changes. Run three conditions, each with two seeds:

- **L1, balanced pool split after copying.** Oversample every class by offline random augmentation to about 1,805 images, giving about 9,000 copies, then split 70/15/15 at random at the copy level.
- **L2, uniform pool split after copying.** Make three offline copies of every image, then split at the copy level. This separates sibling leakage from class balancing.
- **C, clean control.** Split by source image first, reusing the existing 70/15/15 split, then generate the same copies inside training only.

Report test QWK and accuracy overall and separately for test copies with and without a sibling in training. Training sets of about 6,300–7,700 images cost roughly 60–75 s per epoch on a T4, scaled from our measured 25 s per epoch for B3 300 on 2,562 images. Twenty epochs therefore take about 20–25 minutes per run, and the six runs take about 2–2.5 h. The experiment builds new splits over all 3,662 images, including the opened test images. That is legitimate for a protocol study, because it makes no claim about model quality, but the write-up must say so. Run it first, because no other experiment can change the conclusion of the reproduction.

**Experiment 2 design.** The EyePACS arm most likely failed because of its fine-tuning schedule, not because of EyePACS. Our fine-tune restarted at a peak lr of 3e-4 after a 1-epoch warm-up, and OOF fell in epochs 5–7 of every fold. The best-documented APTOS recipe instead pre-trains at 5e-4 and fine-tunes at **1e-5** with cosine decay and no warm-up. Its author reported that warm-up was "not helpful on my local CV" ([Qishen Ha, 4th place](https://www.kaggle.com/c/aptos2019-blindness-detection/discussion/107987)). The winner dropped from 2e-4 to 2e-5 for the final epochs ([Guanshuo Xu, 1st place](https://www.kaggle.com/c/aptos2019-blindness-detection/discussion/108065)). Our peak is therefore 30× above Qishen Ha's fine-tune lr. The early dip fits the finding that full fine-tuning "distort[s] the pretrained features" ([Kumar et al., ICLR 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10054)).

The Stage 4c kernel saved the EMA weights of the 5-epoch pre-train (`eyepacs_pretrain/…_e5.pt`). If the Kaggle outputs are still retained, the fix needs no new pre-training and no EyePACS loading. It does not, however, have the optimizer moments that one gold team said "made a big difference" ([JIANJIAN, 12th](https://www.kaggle.com/c/aptos2019-blindness-detection/discussion/107995)). The run has three steps:

1. Log the zero-shot QWK of the pre-trained weights on APTOS.
2. Probe one fold at lr {1e-5, 3e-5, 1e-4}, cosine, no warm-up restart, 12–15 epochs (about 45 min).
3. Run 5 folds at the winning lr (about 75–90 min).

Keep expectations modest. Gold teams pre-trained for **15–80 epochs**, mostly on about 88,000 labelled 2015 images including the 2015 test set. Our pre-train was 5 epochs on 35,108 images ([Kaggle write-ups, 107926](https://www.kaggle.com/c/aptos2019-blindness-detection/discussion/107926)). The paper's own controlled row moves only 0.954 → 0.956 with EyePACS ([PMC11323616](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11323616/)). Kaggle CVs after 2015 data reach 0.927–0.935 ([Xuan Cao, 17th](https://www.kaggle.com/c/aptos2019-blindness-detection/discussion/107916)). The experiment settles a failed prediction (Q4) and tells the next session whether longer pre-training is worth about 2.5 h. It cannot close a 0.036 gap, and its result never enters the headline.

**Experiment 3 design.** Pseudo-labelling is the only remaining lever that adds new information rather than another correlated view of the same 3,112 labels. All top-4 private-leaderboard teams used it, but none published a before/after number ([U-Tokyo Med summary](https://speakerdeck.com/hoxomaxwell/kaggle-aptos-2019-at-u-tokyo-med)). Single-model private-leaderboard gains were +0.003 to +0.009 ([Camaro, 13th](https://www.kaggle.com/c/aptos2019-blindness-detection/discussion/107944)). That value came largely from adapting to a shifted test distribution: public-leaderboard QWK sat near 0.83 while private reached 0.93. Our random-split test does not have that shift. On ultra-wide-field fundus images, pseudo-labelling actually hurt DR grading ([arXiv 2309.10619](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.10619)).

Fold safety is the critical detail. The student for fold k must be trained on pseudo-labels from the fold-k teachers only, or its OOF score will leak. The run has three steps:

1. Label the 1,928 images with the existing fold-k teachers using soft regression targets, which takes about 15–40 minutes of inference.
2. Optionally down-weight images whose predictions sit near a cut-off.
3. Warm-start B3 384 and B4-NS 380 students from the teacher weights for 10 epochs, about 2.5 h.

A +0.004 median would put OOF near 0.932, but the test set cannot confirm it. Run this third, and cut it first if Experiments 1 and 2 overrun.

## Conclusion

The reproduction has reached its natural endpoint as an engineering exercise. The open question is now forensic: what kind of evaluation produces 0.954 QWK and 94.2% accuracy from a recipe that scores 0.827 and 77% under a strict split? The pairing of the two numbers, not QWK alone, narrows the answers to a mis-reported metric or a test set that overlaps training at the level of near-copies. Every effect we can legitimately attribute to model choice, cut-offs, subsets, duplicates or split luck leaves accuracy stuck in the low 0.80s. That matches the rest of the carefully evaluated literature. A useful rule for reading any APTOS claim follows. A 5-class accuracy above about 0.90 at QWK near 0.95 is a reason to ask for the split and the confusion matrix before treating the number as a benchmark.

The most valuable output of the last nine GPU-hours is therefore a measurement, not a score. Our pipeline can show directly whether splitting after augmentation turns the paper's own recipe into 0.95/0.94. A positive result would turn an inference into a demonstrated mechanism. A negative result would leave reporting error as the main explanation. Either outcome, combined with a request to the authors for the APTOS split and confusion matrix, says more about the 0.954 than any further blend member.
